* Purpose

This document is the onboarding for the deflation pipeline that produces the
=NIOTs_resultados.rdata= consumed by the growth-gap diagnosis.
It fixes the current-versus-legacy boundary so that no diagnosis rests on stale
code or data, and it derives the algebraic decomposition of the gap that the
diagnosis tests.

* The authoritative pipeline

The runnable pipeline lives in =etc/Modelo/=, orchestrated by
=etc/Modelo/compilacao.R=, which sources, in order:

1. =base/titulos.R= — sector titles and the 45-to-7 aggregator.
2. =base/deflator.R= — national GDP deflator, OCDE primary, World Bank fallback.
3. =base/exchange_rate.R= — local currency per United States dollar, OCDE primary, World Bank fallback.
4. =base/base.R= — consolidates the combined deflator and loads the raw national tables.
5. =base/matrizes.R= — applies the deflator and builds the deflated national tables.
6. =base/coeficientes_tecnicos.R=, =base/matriz_impacto.R=, =base/indicadores_sinteticos.R= — technical coefficients and synthetic indicators.

The result is =NIOTs= (75 countries), saved as =etc/Modelo/NIOTs_resultados.rdata=.
The analysis layer =code/support_functions.R= (function =prepare_data()=) consumes
this object and builds the GDP level whose log-difference is $g^{IO}$.

* How the deflation works

The raw OECD Inter-Country Input-Output cells (=NATIODOMIMP=, OECD ICIO national
domestic and import tables, 1995 to 2020) are in current United States dollars.
For each country and year the pipeline forms a single scalar

: deflator_geral = exchange_rate / deflator_GDP        (base.R)

where =exchange_rate= is local currency per dollar and =deflator_GDP= is the
national GDP deflator with base 1995.
Every cell of every matrix is multiplied by this one scalar

: Jull = Meepo * deflator                              (Un.R, Um.R)

so the deflator is uniform across all sectors and all final-demand components
within a country-year.
The output is expressed in constant-1995 local currency
(=matrizes.R= header: "deflacionadas a preços locais e constantes de 1995").

The single uniform deflator is the central fact for the diagnosis.
There are no component-specific deflators, so the residual gap cannot arise from
component-level deflator heterogeneity.

* The gap decomposition

Because the deflator is uniform, the constant-price GDP factors as
$\text{GDP}^{\text{real}}_t = f_t \cdot \text{GDP}^{\text{nom,LCU}}_t$ with
$f_t = \text{exchange\_rate}_t / \text{deflator\_GDP}_t$ and
$\text{GDP}^{\text{nom,LCU}}_t = \text{exchange\_rate}_t \cdot \text{GDP}^{\text{nom,USD}}_t$.
Taking log-differences,

$$g^{IO} = \Delta\ln \text{GDP}^{\text{nom,USD}} + \Delta\ln \text{exchange\_rate} - \Delta\ln \text{deflator\_GDP}.$$

Official real growth is $g^{off} = \Delta\ln \text{GDP}^{\text{nom,LCU}}_{\text{off}} - \Delta\ln \text{deflator}_{\text{off}}$.
Subtracting,

$$\text{Gap} = \underbrace{\big[\Delta\ln \text{GDP}^{\text{nom,LCU}}_{IO} - \Delta\ln \text{GDP}^{\text{nom,LCU}}_{\text{off}}\big]}_{\text{nominal-aggregate mismatch}} + \underbrace{\big[\Delta\ln \text{deflator}_{\text{off}} - \Delta\ln \text{deflator\_GDP}_{\text{used}}\big]}_{\text{deflator mismatch}}.$$

The gap has exactly two sources, both testable from inputs already on disk without
re-running the pipeline:

- the nominal final-demand aggregate from the World Bank tables (in local currency)
  against the official nominal GDP, and
- the GDP deflator used (OCDE/World Bank) against the deflator implicit in the
  official constant series =NY.GDP.MKTP.KD=.

** Consequence for the spike regime

The exchange-rate term $\Delta\ln \text{exchange\_rate}$ enters $g^{IO}$ directly.
A one-time regime change in the official rate therefore injects a one-time jump.
Verified: Myanmar's rate moves 5.44 to 640.65 between 2011 and 2012 (factor 117.7,
$\Delta\ln = 4.77$), reproducing the observed 2012 spike; Nigeria's rate moves
21.89 to 92.34 in 1999 (factor 4.22, $\Delta\ln = 1.44$).
The break lives in the exchange-rate /input/ to the deflation, not in the raw
final-demand volumes.
This corrects the earlier attribution to =code/deflate_tables.R= (the incomplete
refactor, whose deflation is stubbed) and to the raw =Ft= components.

* Data provenance and fork lineage (review 2026-07-21)

The repository is a fork.
The upstream project (initial commit by Quentin Perrier, 2019) deflates the World
Input-Output Database (WIOD, 2016 release).
This fork (from 2025) repurposes that deflation code but changes the input source:
Stage A now reads the OECD Inter-Country Input-Output tables from
=inputs/NATIODOMIMP/= (1995 to 2020, 76 countries) and produces
=etc/Modelo/NIOTs_resultados.rdata= (75 countries).
The WIOD deflation is retained as /heritage/, documented as such in =README.md=,
and produces the separate =results/= artifacts (=wiot_*_deflated.rds=,
=results/NIOTs_resultados.rdata= with 43 countries, the WIOD-2016 country set).
The heritage scripts are =code/price_deflator.R= and =code/checks.R=.

Consequence for the diagnosis: the term "WIOD" for the analysed volumes is
inherited from upstream and is incorrect.
The diagnosis loads =etc/Modelo/NIOTs_resultados.rdata=, which is built from the
OECD Inter-Country Input-Output tables; the countries that carry the residual gap
(Myanmar, Nigeria, Viet Nam, Ukraine, Bangladesh, Brunei, Kazakhstan, Egypt,
Morocco, Pakistan, Belarus, Côte d'Ivoire) exist only in the OECD set and are
absent from WIOD.
No conclusion depends on the WIOD heritage: the diagnosis never loads =results/=,
never runs =checks.R= or =price_deflator.R=, and the currency and deflator inputs
it reads are byte-identical to those the OECD Stage-A pipeline used.
The findings and this onboarding were corrected on 2026-07-21 to name the source
OECD Inter-Country Input-Output rather than WIOD; the numeric results are
unchanged because they were always computed on the OECD-built object.

* Two versions of the pipeline

The project holds two versions of the same deflation pipeline.

- The /original/ pipeline, =etc/Modelo/base/*.R= (mirrored, in part, under
  =code/base/=), is runnable and produced the loaded data.
- The /refactor/, =code/*.R= (not =code/base/=), is an updated rewrite of that
  original: =deflate_tables.R= rewrites =matrizes.R= with =Un.R=, =Um.R= and
  =base.R=; =price_deflator.R= rewrites =deflator.R=; =support_functions.R=
  rewrites the analysis and synthetic indicators.

The analysis layer of the refactor (=support_functions.R=, =prepare_data()=) is
complete and in use; it consumed the data the original pipeline produced.
The Stage-A deflation of the refactor is /incomplete/: in =deflate_tables.R= the
component sources are commented behind a =## FIXME= (lines 82 to 85) and the
deflator multiplication is disabled (lines 94 and 103,
=Jull <- Meepo # * deflator=), so the refactor would emit undeflated tables and
does not yet reproduce the original.
=code/tmp.R= is an earlier refactor draft with a syntax error at line 391.

* Open hypotheses this onboarding sharpens

- H-F, deflator mismatch.
  The gap term $\Delta\ln \text{deflator}_{\text{off}} - \Delta\ln \text{deflator\_GDP}_{\text{used}}$
  is nonzero when the OCDE/World Bank GDP deflator differs from the deflator
  implicit in =NY.GDP.MKTP.KD=.
  Test: form the implicit official deflator as =NY.GDP.MKTP.CN= over
  =NY.GDP.MKTP.KN= (nominal over real, local currency), compare its log-change to
  the =deflator_GDP= used, and correlate the difference with the gap.

- H-G, price-concept mismatch.
  =base/deflator.R= labels the OCDE series "deflator do GDP a preços do
  consumidor", a consumer-price basis, while the OECD Inter-Country Input-Output
  tables are valued at basic prices and official GDP is at market prices.
  A basis mismatch would appear as a systematic component of the deflator-mismatch
  term, concentrated where the wedge between consumer and market prices moves
  (indirect taxes, subsidies, terms of trade).
  Test: for a high-dispersion country, replace the applied deflator with the
  market-price GDP deflator and observe whether the gap contracts.

- H-nominal, nominal-aggregate mismatch.
  The OECD Inter-Country Input-Output nominal final demand (in local currency)
  may not track official nominal GDP.
  Test: compare $\Delta\ln \text{GDP}^{\text{nom,LCU}}_{IO}$ with
  $\Delta\ln$ =NY.GDP.MKTP.CN= per country-year.

The two decomposition terms are additive and jointly exhaust the gap, so
attributing each country-year's gap between them is the next diagnostic step and
the path from ninety to ninety-five percent confidence.
