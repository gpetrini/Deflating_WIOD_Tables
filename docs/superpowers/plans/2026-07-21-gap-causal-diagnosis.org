#+title: Plan: causal diagnosis of the gap, by hypothesis and confidence
#+date: 2026-07-21
#+filetags: :IODefl:diagnosis:plan:

* Purpose

This plan executes =docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-21-gap-diagnosis-revised.org=
under the =/diagnose= discipline.
The spec orders the measurements; this plan orders the hypotheses.
Every hypothesis carries a confidence level and the evidence that would raise it
to ninety-five percent.
No hypothesis is accepted or rejected below that level.

* Phase 1: the feedback loop

The =/diagnose= discipline holds that the loop is the work and the rest is
mechanical.
The loop for this problem is an identity rather than a test.

Stage A multiplies every cell of the raw current-dollar table by one scalar per
country-year, $e / P$, and GDP is a linear combination of components that all
carry that scalar, so the scalar passes through the aggregation unchanged:

$$g^{IO} = \Delta\ln(\text{ICIO nominal, USD}) + \Delta\ln e - \pi$$

and therefore

$$\mathrm{Gap} = \underbrace{\Delta\ln(\text{ICIO nominal, USD})}_{\text{fixed}}
+ \underbrace{\Delta\ln e}_{\text{swappable}}
- \underbrace{\pi}_{\text{swappable}}
- \underbrace{g^{off}}_{\text{swappable}}.$$

Three of the four terms can be replaced from files already on disk without
re-running Stage A, which matters because the deflation in
=code/deflate_tables.R= is stubbed and cannot reproduce the supplied object.
Only the ICIO nominal aggregate is fixed.

This yields a deterministic pass, running in seconds, that answers "what would
the gap have been under series X" for any candidate series.
It was verified this session: substituting the deflator through the identity
reproduces the panel exactly where the series coincide.

The loop's limit is the counterpart: no hypothesis about the ICIO nominal
aggregate can be tested by substitution, because there is nothing to substitute.
That term is closed only by an external comparison, which is H1 below.

* Phase 2: reproduction

Reproduction is established and is not repeated.
The panel spans 1996 to 2020 over seventy-four countries, 1850 country-years,
against the current benchmark; the former benchmark is retained behind a switch
in =get_gOff()=.
The symptom is a median absolute gap near 0.4 percentage points with a tail
reaching several points in a minority of countries.

* Phase 3: the ranked hypotheses

Confidence below is confidence in the stated verdict, not in the hypothesis
being true.

** H1: nominal-aggregate mismatch. Confidence 90, verdict pending

The ICIO final-demand aggregate does not equal official nominal GDP, because the
tables are balanced and estimated rather than transcribed.

Prediction: the gap survives every substitution of $e$, $P$ and $g^{off}$, and
the surviving residual tracks the difference between the ICIO nominal aggregate
and official nominal GDP.

Standing: it carries forty-two percent of gap variance over the sixty-eight
non-spike countries by covariance attribution, against forty-five percent under
the former benchmark, so the attribution is stable across the benchmark
revision.

To reach ninety-five: compare the ICIO nominal dollar aggregate against official
nominal GDP in levels, per country-year, not through the variance attribution.
The comparison is the direct test and has not been run.
The obstacle is that the World Bank current-dollar file is the same 2018 release
and ends in 2016, so the direct test is available only to 2016 unless a
current-dollar series reaching 2020 is located on disk.

** H2: applied-deflator error, OECD-covered countries. Confidence 90, verdict rejection

For the forty-seven countries deflated with Economic Outlook 116, the applied
deflator is not the source of the gap.

Prediction: substituting =NY.GDP.DEFL.ZS= leaves the gap unchanged or worsens it.

Standing: the substitution moves the mean absolute gap from 0.634 to 0.691
percentage points, an increase of nine percent, against a pre-registered
threshold of twenty percent for refutation.
The median-based counterpart moves one percent.
The direction matters: the pre-registered limit that a contraction would be
partly mechanical, since the substituted deflator shares a provider with the
benchmark, does not apply to an increase.

Why it is not yet ninety-five: the pooled mean conceals country-level movement
of opposite sign and much larger size, from minus thirty-two percent for Brazil
to plus three hundred percent for Colombia.
A statistic that unstable across countries is a weak basis for a pooled verdict,
whatever its point value.

To reach ninety-five: restate the decision at the country level, which the
pre-registered rule did not anticipate.
Count the countries whose absolute gap falls by more than twenty percent under
the substitution.
If that count is small, the rejection holds for the group and not merely for its
average.
Then establish why the substitution worsens Colombia, Turkey, Indonesia and
Argentina, since a deflator substitution that worsens the fit by a factor is
evidence about those series and must not be left unexplained.

** H3: applied-deflator error, World Bank fallback countries. Confidence 50, no verdict

For the twenty-nine countries deflated with =NY.GDP.DEFL.ZS=, including every
drifter and every spike country, the applied deflator is untested.

Prediction: substituting an independent local-currency deflator removes the
cumulative drift in Ukraine, Brunei, Kazakhstan, Viet Nam and Egypt.

Standing: untestable by the loop as constituted.
Deflator provenance is collinear with drift, so there is no second
local-currency deflator on disk for these countries.
The deflator mismatch term carries between sixty-six and ninety-seven percent of
the gap for the five drifters, stable across the benchmark revision, but that
figure identifies a term in an identity and not a cause.

To reach ninety-five: a third local-currency deflator covering the five
drifters, on the same base and window.
The only candidate on disk,
=inputs/price_deflators/unsd_snaAma_GDP_ImplicitPriceDeflator_USD.txt=, ends in
2016 and is expressed in dollars, so it reintroduces the exchange rate and must
be used either in dollar space on both sides or divided by the same rate the
deflation applies.
Any other source requires a download, which the maintainer has not authorised.
Until one of the two is settled, H3 has no verdict.

** H4: exchange-rate series error. Confidence 85, verdict rejection

Prediction: substituting the World Bank exchange rate for the OECD rate leaves
the gap unchanged.

Standing: the regression of the gap on $\Delta\ln e$ returned a coefficient near
zero, which refuted the conjecture that the series is nominal.
That test answers a different question from this one.
The identity places $\Delta\ln e$ in $g^{IO}$ with a coefficient of one, so an
error in the level or timing of the rate enters the growth rate directly, and a
coefficient near zero on the gap does not exclude it.

To reach ninety-five: run the exchange-rate substitution through the same
identity used for the deflator.
Both series are on disk, both cover the window, and the substitution has never
been run.
This is the cheapest outstanding test in the plan and should be executed first.

** H5: spike contamination of the pooled statistics. Confidence 95, verdict accepted

Seven countries carry one-time multiplicative re-expressions of every
final-demand component, inherited from the supplied national accounts.

Standing: verified at the level of the raw table for Myanmar and Nigeria, where
the jump factor matches the exchange-rate change and domestic inflation does not
account for it.
The consequence is quantified: pooled attribution including the seven gives
seven percent to the nominal-aggregate term, against forty-two percent when they
are excluded, so the seven countries determine the pooled result.

Action, not further testing: exclude them by a written rule in every pooled
statistic, and report them separately.
The exclusion list must be fixed once and cited, so that it is not re-derived
per table.

** H6: benchmark vintage. Confidence 95, verdict accepted and quantified

Standing: settled.
The revision between the 2018 release and the 2026 download has a mean near zero
and a standard deviation of one percentage point.
It accounts for over ninety percent of the previously measured mean absolute gap
for Saudi Arabia, Latvia and Luxembourg, and eighty-five percent for Mexico,
while moving the aggregate mean absolute gap only from 1.243 to 1.134 percentage
points.

Consequence for the record: every country-level claim made against the former
benchmark is withdrawn unless reproduced.
The aggregate claims survive.

** H7: deflator vintage. Confidence 50, no verdict

Economic Outlook 116 is applied to volumes from the 2023 ICIO release, so the
deflator and the tables may embed different benchmark revisions.

To reach ninety-five: a second vintage of the same deflator, which is not on
disk.
Without it, H7 cannot be separated from H2, and any rejection of H2 is a
rejection of the pair rather than of the deflator alone.
This limit must be stated wherever H2's verdict is cited.

** H8: 2020 as a distinct regime. Confidence 70, no verdict

Standing: the median absolute gap is 0.333 percentage points over 2017 to 2019
and 0.795 in 2020, against 0.396 over 1996 to 2016.
The three pre-pandemic years behave like the estimated window, which is itself a
result, since it shows the repointing did not import a new regime.

Why it is not ninety-five: the comparison was made after the data were seen, so
no threshold was pre-registered, and a doubling of a median over one year across
seventy-four countries is consistent with a demand composition shift that any
fixed-weight aggregation would produce.

To reach ninety-five: state whether 2020 is to be diagnosed or excluded before
examining it further.
If diagnosed, the prediction is specific: a fixed-weight aggregation strains
when component shares move, so the 2020 gap should correlate with the size of
the within-country shift in final-demand shares, and not otherwise.
That prediction is testable from the tables alone.

** H9: growth-convention mismatch. Confidence 95, verdict rejected as a cause

The reports use simple growth and the diagnosis uses log growth.
Since the repointing converts the benchmark as $\ln(1+g/100)$ with a round-trip
error of order $10^{-15}$, both sides of the gap are now on one convention, and
the mismatch cannot contribute.

It remains a reporting inconsistency between =support_functions.R= and the
diagnosis, and is recorded as such rather than as a cause.

* Phase 4: order of execution

The order is chosen so that each step either closes a hypothesis or sharpens the
one after it.

1. H4, the exchange-rate substitution.
   Cheapest, uses the existing loop, and closes the last term of the identity
   that has never been substituted.
   Until it runs, any residual attributed to the nominal aggregate may be an
   exchange-rate artefact.

2. H5, fix the exclusion list in writing.
   Every pooled statistic after this point depends on it.

3. H2, restate at the country level and explain the four countries the
   substitution worsens.

4. H1, the direct level comparison against official nominal GDP, restricted to
   the window the current-dollar series permits.
   This is the hypothesis the loop cannot reach by substitution and is where the
   diagnosis is most likely to terminate.

5. H3 and H7, both blocked on data that is either absent or currency-mismatched.
   Report the block rather than substituting a weaker test.

6. H8, only after a decision on whether 2020 is in scope.

* Success criteria

Each hypothesis above carries either a verdict at ninety-five percent confidence
or a written statement of the evidence that would produce one, and no statement
of cause rests on a term of an identity being large.

* Note on method

An identity does not attribute cause.
The decomposition into nominal-aggregate and deflator mismatch is an algebraic
rearrangement, so a term being large establishes where the gap sits in the
identity and not what produced it.
Every verdict above therefore rests on a substitution or an external comparison,
and never on the size of a term alone.
