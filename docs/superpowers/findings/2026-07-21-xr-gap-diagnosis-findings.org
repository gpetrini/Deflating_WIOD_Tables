* Purpose

This document records the empirical verdict of the growth-gap diagnosis and the
decision it motivates, for the co-authors.
It supersedes the working conjecture stated in the diagnosis spec.
Numbers correspond to the panel built at commit on branch =diagnosis/xr-gap-debug=
(=code/diagnose_xr_gap.R=; panel persisted at =results/diagnostics/gap_panel.rds=).

* Object

The gap is defined per country-year as $\text{Gap} = g^{IO} - g^{off}$.
$g^{IO}$ is the log-difference of the GDP level constructed in =prepare_data()=
from the supplied =NIOTs_resultados.rdata=.
$g^{off}$ is the log-difference of the World Bank constant-dollar series
=NY.GDP.MKTP.KD=.
The panel covers 1575 country-years, 75 countries, 1996 to 2016.

* Verdict on the exchange-rate and inflation conjecture

This verdict covers the original conjecture only.
It is not a verdict on the cause of the residual gap, which remains open.

The original conjecture is refuted.
The divergence between IO-computed and official real GDP growth is not driven by
the conversion of local-currency values into United States dollars.
Neither the exchange rate nor domestic inflation explains the gap systematically.

The two-regressor fit $\text{Gap} \sim \Delta\ln e + \pi + \text{factor(ISO)}$
gives the following.

| Sample                | $\beta_e$ | $\beta_\pi$ | within-$R^2$ |
|-----------------------+-----------+-------------+--------------|
| Full                  |    -0.629 |      -0.690 |        0.613 |
| Trimmed $|Gap|\le 0.5$ |    -0.078 |      -0.167 |        0.121 |
| Robust (rlm)          |    -0.012 |      -0.025 |            — |

The full-sample coefficients are leverage from five observations.
Removing them collapses both coefficients toward zero, and a robust estimator
returns $\beta_e \approx 0$ and $\beta_\pi \approx 0$.
The correlation $\text{cor}(\text{Gap}, \Delta\ln e)$ falls from $-0.625$ on the
full sample to $0.002$ after trimming those five points.

* Deflation state of the supplied series

The supplied series behaves as real, not nominal.
A nominal-local-currency series would satisfy $\text{Gap} \approx \pi$, so
domestic inflation would carry a coefficient near one.
The estimate is $\beta_\pi \approx 0$ and $\text{cor}(\text{Gap}, \pi) \approx 0$.
The object was therefore deflated before it was supplied, although the
conversion line at =code/deflate_tables.R:94= is commented out; the supplied
object was produced by a path other than the current Stage-A checkout.

* The residual gap is a data-quality phenomenon, in two regimes

Against the benchmark that a correct deflation reproduces official real growth,
every non-trivial gap is a defect.
The gap is tight at the core and has a wide tail.

| Threshold | Share of country-years above |
|-----------+------------------------------|
| 0.5 p.p.  | 47.9%                        |
| 1 p.p.    | 27.6%                        |
| 2 p.p.    | 12.3%                        |
| 5 p.p.    | 3.8%                         |
| 50 p.p.   | 0.3% (5 obs)                 |

Classifying each country by the mean and the standard deviation of its gap over
the sample separates three regimes; a fourth candidate, a persistent constant
offset, is absent.

| Regime   | Countries | Signature                                   | Examples                     |
|----------+-----------+---------------------------------------------+------------------------------|
| Clean    |        56 | gap near zero every year, sd below 2 p.p.   | JPN (sd 0.002), FRA, AUT, THA |
| Volatile |        12 | mean near zero, sd 3 to 7 p.p.              | VNM, UKR, BGD, KAZ, BGR, NZL |
| Spike    |         7 | one-time level discontinuity dominates      | MMR, NGA, MLT, CYP           |
| Offset   |         0 | constant nonzero mean, small sd             | none                         |

No country carries a persistent constant offset.
The deflation reproduces official growth for 56 of the 75 countries.

** Spike: one-time discontinuity at an exchange-rate regime change

The GDP level jumps once, by a factor close to the exchange-rate change.
Myanmar rises from 20,387 in 2011 to 2,485,941 in 2012, a factor near 122,
against $\Delta\ln e = -4.77$; the resulting $g^{IO} = 4.80 \approx -\Delta\ln e$.
Nigeria rises from 881,635 in 1998 to 3,345,492 in 1999, a factor near 3.8,
against $\Delta\ln e = -1.44$.
The raw national tables re-express values at the new official rate in the break
year, and the level discontinuity enters the growth rate once.
Domestic inflation does not account for the jump (Myanmar 2012 $\pi = 0.031$).

*** Spike sub-audit (2026-07-21): root cause and hybrid case

The break is a one-time multiplicative re-scaling of every final-demand
component in the raw =Ft= table at a single year, inherited from the supplied
national accounts, not introduced by the pipeline.
Aggregating =Ft= by year confirms the level jump; decomposing it by component
shows the four non-inventory components (HFCE, GGFC, GFCF, EXPO) share a common
jump factor, while inventories (INVNT), being small and sign-changing, carry no
information about the scale change.

The decisive test is whether the two segments on either side of the break are
each internally clean.
Splicing a single break repairs a country only when both segments already track
official growth.

| ISO | Break | Break Gap | pre mean\vert Gap\vert | post mean\vert Gap\vert | Jump factor (non-INVNT) | Mechanism         |
|-----+-------+-----------+------------------------+-------------------------+-------------------------+-------------------|
| NGA |  1999 |     1.329 | edge                   |                   0.027 | 3.6                     | FX re-expression  |
| MLT |  2008 |     0.860 |                  0.022 |                   0.012 | 2.5                     | euro adoption     |
| CYP |  2008 |     0.538 |                  0.005 |                   0.011 | 1.8                     | euro adoption     |
| BLR |  1996 |    -0.300 | edge                   |                   0.014 | 0.75                    | redenomination    |
| CIV |  1996 |    -0.409 | edge                   |                   0.030 | 0.75                    | vintage/rescale   |
| PAK |  2000 |    -0.485 |                  0.011 |                   0.026 | 0.66                    | redenomination    |
| MMR |  2012 |     4.733 |                  0.173 |                   0.075 | 126                     | FX + pre-2012 vol |

Six of the seven are clean single-break spikes: both segments track official
growth, so removing the single corrupted break-year growth zeroes the gap.
Myanmar is the exception.
Its 2012 jump is a genuine FX re-expression, but its entire pre-2012 segment is
volatile (mean gap 0.173 against 0.075 after), so a splice cannot repair it.
Myanmar is therefore a hybrid: a spike at 2012 over a volatile pre-2012 base,
and its pre-2012 years route to the volatile-regime investigation.

The mechanism splits three ways and does not always coincide with the exchange
rate: NGA and MMR match $-\Delta\ln e$; MLT and CYP are euro adoption; BLR, CIV
and PAK are redenominations or vintage rescales whose factor $\Delta\ln e$ does
not explain (PAK is a textbook uniform rescale, all components times 0.66).

**** Hypotheses and the evidence required for 95 percent confidence

- H-A, break mechanism.
  Each break is a one-time common re-scaling of the raw =Ft= components at a
  single year.
  To reach 95 percent confidence: the four non-inventory components share a jump
  factor within a coefficient of variation below 0.15; that factor matches an
  /independent/ external source (official rate ratio for an FX break, documented
  redenomination factor otherwise) within about ten percent; and both adjacent
  segments carry mean $\vert Gap\vert$ below 0.03.
  The external source must not be the OCDE exchange-rate file already used to
  compute $\Delta\ln e$, or the match is circular.

- H-B, Myanmar is hybrid.
  Splicing the 2012 break leaves Myanmar's pre-2012 mean $\vert Gap\vert$ above
  0.05, confirming a residual data-quality problem that the splice does not
  address.

- H-C, splice sufficiency.
  For the six clean spikes, after the splice the break-year $\vert Gap\vert$
  falls below 0.02, the neighbouring years are unchanged, and the robust
  =fit_gap_model= coefficients stay near zero.

** Volatile: mean-zero annual dispersion

The gap fluctuates year to year with a mean near zero and a large standard
deviation, so the multi-year average growth is right while single years err.
New Zealand has a mean gap of 0.002 with a standard deviation of 0.038, swinging
between $+0.058$ and $-0.068$ across adjacent years.
The regime concentrates in transition and emerging economies (Viet Nam, Ukraine,
Bangladesh, Kazakhstan, Bulgaria, Romania), which points to year-to-year noise in
the component deflators or import shares of those IO tables, not a fixed bias.

*** Pervasive-gap diagnosis (2026-07-21): what the residual is not

    :STATUS: partially superseded, see "Panel reconciliation and provenance corrections"
    :END:

Setting the seven exogenous break countries aside, the gap over the remaining
sixty-eight countries is unbiased and small at the centre with a heavy tail.
The signed mean is $-0.0009$ (t $=-1.58$, no systematic bias); the standard
deviation is $0.021$; forty-four percent of country-years exceed half a
percentage point and nine percent exceed two.

Three candidate pipeline causes were tested and rejected.

| Candidate cause                     | Test                                   | Result                          | Verdict   |
|-------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+---------------------------------+-----------|
| Import-content adjustment $(1-m)$   | mean$\vert Gap\vert$ with vs without   | 0.009 with, 0.020 without       | improves  |
| Index-number formula (sum vs chain) | Törnqvist of same components           | differ by 0.0005; both 0.009    | rejected  |
| Within-country component dispersion | cor($\vert Gap\vert$, growth sd)       | 0.025, $R^2=0.001$              | rejected  |

The import adjustment reduces the gap rather than causing it, the aggregation
formula is immaterial, and heterogeneous component growth does not predict the
residual.
The construction reproduces official growth to about two tenths of a percentage
point for well-measured economies (Thailand, Austria, Korea, France, Japan all
below 0.003 standard deviation) and diverges by three to seven percentage points
for a dozen transition and emerging economies, a thirty-fold range driven by the
country, not the method.

The annual gap is mean-reverting: its average lag-one autocorrelation is
$-0.13$, strongly negative for the noisiest well-behaved cases (New Zealand
$-0.33$, Egypt $-0.42$), so the levels track official over the window even where
single years diverge.
A minority carry a genuine cumulative drift (Ukraine $-0.40$ log points over the
window, Brunei $+0.31$), where the source real-GDP trajectory departs from
official systematically.

The residual therefore lives in the supplied OECD Inter-Country Input-Output
volumes and deflators for these countries, not in the aggregation.
The pipeline is validated by the well-measured economies; the defect is inherited
from the source.

**** Gap decomposition into nominal-aggregate and deflator mismatch

Because the deflation applies a single uniform scalar per country-year, the gap
splits exactly into two additive terms (derivation in
=docs/deflation-pipeline-onboarding.org=):

$$\text{Gap} = \underbrace{[\Delta\ln \text{FD}^{\text{nom,USD}}_{IO} - \Delta\ln \text{GDP}^{\text{nom,USD}}_{\text{off}}]}_{\text{nominal-aggregate mismatch}} + \underbrace{[\Delta\ln \text{deflator}^{USD}_{\text{off}} - \Delta\ln \text{deflator}^{USD}_{\text{pipeline}}]}_{\text{deflator mismatch}}.$$

The reconstruction is exact, using only the panel and World Bank current-dollar
GDP (=NY.GDP.MKTP.CD=); the identity holds to machine precision.
Attributing the variance of the gap by covariance with each term (the shares sum
to one) over the sixty-eight non-spike countries gives forty-five percent to the
nominal-aggregate mismatch and fifty-five percent to the deflator mismatch.
Both terms are individually larger than the gap and offset, so neither alone is
the story; the residual is what fails to cancel.

Per country the dominant term separates the regime.
The deflator mismatch drives the countries with a cumulative drift: Ukraine
(ninety percent deflator, cumulative deflator term $-0.30$ of a $-0.40$ total
gap), Brunei (ninety-eight percent, $+0.41$), Kazakhstan (eighty-nine percent),
and Viet Nam (sixty-six percent).
The nominal-aggregate mismatch drives Bulgaria, Romania, Tunisia, Russia and New
Zealand, where the OECD Inter-Country Input-Output final-demand aggregate departs
from official nominal GDP.
The systematic country drifts are therefore a deflator phenomenon, and the
year-to-year dispersion of the more stable countries is a nominal-aggregate
phenomenon.

Function: =decompose_gap()= and =attribute_gap()= in =code/diagnose_xr_gap.R=.

**** Hypotheses and the evidence required for 95 percent confidence

- H-D, the residual is source data quality. *Open, not confirmed.*
  The gap is not produced by the import adjustment, the index formula, or
  component-growth dispersion, and it concentrates by country.
  That evidence reaches about ninety percent, below the bar, and it cannot
  distinguish an inherited defect in the supplied volumes from the choice of
  applied deflator, which is the fork stated in =CONTEXT.org=.
  Do not cite this as a verdict.

- H-E, mean-reverting measurement noise versus cumulative drift.
  For most high-dispersion countries the annual gap reverts (negative
  autocorrelation, cumulative drift below the independent-noise benchmark of
  $\text{sd}\sqrt{n}$); for a minority (Ukraine, Brunei) it accumulates.
  Reaching 95 percent confidence requires separating the two by testing whether
  the cumulative drift of each country exceeds its independent-noise band.

- H-F, deflator choice versus source volumes (open).
  The mean-reverting noise is consistent either with a national deflator that is
  itself noisy for high-inflation transition economies or with noisy OECD
  Inter-Country Input-Output volume estimates.
  The discriminating test is to re-deflate one high-dispersion country (Ukraine
  or Viet Nam) with the national GDP deflator applied uniformly and observe
  whether the annual gap collapses; a collapse implicates the deflator, its
  persistence implicates the source volumes.
  This is the path to raising the diagnosis from ninety to ninety-five percent
  and to deciding whether the residual is reducible.

* Decision

The diagnosis has answered its causal question, and the productive work shifts.
The remaining spec tasks built on the exchange-rate-versus-inflation framing
(counterfactual, residual attribution across H2 to H4, confidence statement) are
no longer the object, because that framing is resolved.
The next work is a per-country and per-year gap audit whose target is to drive
the gap toward zero across the base, treating each gap above a threshold as a
defect and separating the regimes above.
The 56 clean countries need no further work.
Six of the seven spike countries (NGA, MLT, CYP, BLR, CIV, PAK) require splicing
a single break year; the splice method is the open decision (drop the
unmeasurable break-year growth, or rescale one segment by an external factor).
Myanmar requires the splice and, separately, the volatile-regime treatment for
its pre-2012 segment.
The 12 volatile countries require tracing the year-to-year dispersion into the
component deflators and import shares of their IO tables.

* Hypothesis review after the provenance correction (2026-07-21)

The provenance correction (the analysed volumes are OECD ICIO, not WIOD) does not
overturn any result, but it changes which hypotheses may be retired and which
must stay open.

Retired hypotheses stay retired, because their tests are source-agnostic.
The series is real not nominal ($\beta_\pi\approx 0$), the exchange-rate level
does not explain the gap ($\beta_e\approx 0$ in the discriminating fit), the
base year is growth-invariant, the index-number formula is immaterial, and
within-country component dispersion does not predict the gap.
None of these depends on whether the source is OECD or WIOD.

Hypotheses that must not be dropped, and one to add, given the OECD provenance:

- H-nominal and H-F stay open, jointly explaining the gap (45 and 55 percent).
  OECD ICIO tables are balanced and estimated, so their final-demand aggregate
  need not equal official nominal GDP, which is exactly the nominal-aggregate
  mismatch.

- H-G is elevated, not dropped.
  The chain now spans three price bases: OECD ICIO tables at basic prices, an
  applied deflator labelled consumer prices, and official GDP at market prices.
  A basis mismatch is more plausible here than under a single-source framing.

- H-vintage is added.
  The OECD ICIO edition (1995 to 2020, consistent with the 2023 release) embeds
  SNA benchmark revisions whose vintage may differ from the World Bank GDP series
  the diagnosis compares against.
  A vintage or benchmark mismatch would appear as a persistent per-country level
  drift, which is precisely the signature of the deflator-driven drifters
  (Ukraine, Brunei, Kazakhstan).
  This must be separated from a genuine deflator error before either is claimed.

- The exchange-rate channel is not fully dead.
  Although the gap is not explained by the exchange-rate /level/, the deflation
  multiplies by the exchange rate, so a discrepancy between the OECD/World Bank
  rate used and the rate implicit in the official series is bundled inside the
  deflator-mismatch term and must be separated with the local-currency series.

- The reason the residual concentrates in transition and emerging economies is
  reinforced: these are the OECD-only countries, absent from WIOD, that OECD ICIO
  estimates with weaker national-accounts inputs.

** The substitution identity, which is the feedback loop

Stage A multiplies every cell by one scalar per country-year, $e/P$, and GDP is
a linear combination of components that all carry that scalar, so it passes
through the aggregation unchanged:

$$g^{IO} = \Delta\ln(\text{ICIO nominal, USD}) + \Delta\ln e - \pi.$$

Any candidate exchange rate or deflator can therefore be substituted
analytically, without re-running Stage A, which matters because the deflation in
=code/deflate_tables.R= is stubbed and cannot reproduce the supplied object.
The counterpart is that the ICIO nominal aggregate is not substitutable, so no
hypothesis about it can be closed by this loop.

** Task 4, the four unexamined years

Median absolute gap: 0.396 percentage points over 1996 to 2016, 0.333 over 2017
to 2019, and 0.795 in 2020.
The three pre-pandemic years behave like the estimated window, so the repointing
did not import a new regime.
2020 stands apart, at roughly twice the median of the years before it, with mean
gap $-0.215$: the input-output series grows less than official in the pandemic
year.
No threshold was pre-registered for this comparison, so no verdict is recorded.

** Task 5, the pre-committed deflator swap

Substituting =NY.GDP.DEFL.ZS= for Economic Outlook 116 across the forty-seven
OECD-covered countries moves the mean absolute gap from 0.634 to 0.691
percentage points, an increase of nine percent; the median-based counterpart
moves one percent.
Both fall inside the pre-registered refutation region of twenty percent.

The direction carries information the pre-registration did not anticipate: the
stated limit that a contraction would be partly mechanical, since the
substituted deflator shares a provider with the benchmark, does not apply to an
increase.
The other stated limit stands: the test conflates provider and vintage, so this
is a rejection of the pair and not of the deflator alone.

The verdict is nevertheless withheld at ninety percent.
The pooled mean conceals country movement of opposite sign and larger size,
from $-32$ percent for Brazil and $-28$ for Ireland to $+62$ for Argentina,
$+99$ for Indonesia, $+170$ for Turkey and $+303$ for Colombia.
A pooled mean over a distribution that heterogeneous is a weak basis for a group
verdict whatever its point value, and the four countries the substitution
worsens are themselves evidence about those series that must not be left
unexplained.

** A hypothesis the identity raises

The exchange-rate series has never been substituted.
The regression of the gap on $\Delta\ln e$ returned a coefficient near zero,
which refuted the conjecture that the series is nominal, but the identity places
$\Delta\ln e$ inside $g^{IO}$ with a coefficient of one, so an error in the
level or the dating of the rate enters the growth rate directly and a null
coefficient on the gap does not exclude it.
Both series are on disk and the substitution is the cheapest outstanding test.

** Method

An identity does not attribute cause.
A term of the decomposition being large establishes where the gap sits in the
identity, not what produced it, so every verdict rests on a substitution or an
external comparison and never on the size of a term.
