#+date: 2026-07-21
#+filetags: :IODefl:diagnosis:findings:

* Purpose

This document records the empirical verdict of the growth-gap diagnosis and the
decision it motivates, for the co-authors.
It supersedes the working conjecture stated in the diagnosis spec.
Numbers correspond to the panel built at commit on branch =diagnosis/xr-gap-debug=
(=code/diagnose_xr_gap.R=; panel persisted at =results/diagnostics/gap_panel.rds=).

* Object

The gap is defined per country-year as $\text{Gap} = g^{IO} - g^{off}$.
$g^{IO}$ is the log-difference of the GDP level constructed in =prepare_data()=
from the supplied =NIOTs_resultados.rdata=.
$g^{off}$ is the log-difference of the World Bank constant-dollar series
=NY.GDP.MKTP.KD=.
The panel covers 1575 country-years, 75 countries, 1996 to 2016.

* Verdict

The original conjecture is refuted.
The divergence between IO-computed and official real GDP growth is not driven by
the conversion of local-currency values into United States dollars.
Neither the exchange rate nor domestic inflation explains the gap systematically.

The two-regressor fit $\text{Gap} \sim \Delta\ln e + \pi + \text{factor(ISO)}$
gives the following.

| Sample                | $\beta_e$ | $\beta_\pi$ | within-$R^2$ |
|-----------------------+-----------+-------------+--------------|
| Full                  |    -0.629 |      -0.690 |        0.613 |
| Trimmed $|Gap|\le 0.5$ |    -0.078 |      -0.167 |        0.121 |
| Robust (rlm)          |    -0.012 |      -0.025 |            — |

The full-sample coefficients are leverage from five observations.
Removing them collapses both coefficients toward zero, and a robust estimator
returns $\beta_e \approx 0$ and $\beta_\pi \approx 0$.
The correlation $\text{cor}(\text{Gap}, \Delta\ln e)$ falls from $-0.625$ on the
full sample to $0.002$ after trimming those five points.

* Deflation state of the supplied series

The supplied series behaves as real, not nominal.
A nominal-local-currency series would satisfy $\text{Gap} \approx \pi$, so
domestic inflation would carry a coefficient near one.
The estimate is $\beta_\pi \approx 0$ and $\text{cor}(\text{Gap}, \pi) \approx 0$.
The object was therefore deflated before it was supplied, although the
conversion line at =code/deflate_tables.R:94= is commented out; the supplied
object was produced by a path other than the current Stage-A checkout.

* The residual gap is a data-quality phenomenon, in two regimes

Against the benchmark that a correct deflation reproduces official real growth,
every non-trivial gap is a defect.
The gap is tight at the core and has a wide tail.

| Threshold | Share of country-years above |
|-----------+------------------------------|
| 0.5 p.p.  | 47.9%                        |
| 1 p.p.    | 27.6%                        |
| 2 p.p.    | 12.3%                        |
| 5 p.p.    | 3.8%                         |
| 50 p.p.   | 0.3% (5 obs)                 |

Classifying each country by the mean and the standard deviation of its gap over
the sample separates three regimes; a fourth candidate, a persistent constant
offset, is absent.

| Regime   | Countries | Signature                                   | Examples                     |
|----------+-----------+---------------------------------------------+------------------------------|
| Clean    |        56 | gap near zero every year, sd below 2 p.p.   | JPN (sd 0.002), FRA, AUT, THA |
| Volatile |        12 | mean near zero, sd 3 to 7 p.p.              | VNM, UKR, BGD, KAZ, BGR, NZL |
| Spike    |         7 | one-time level discontinuity dominates      | MMR, NGA, MLT, CYP           |
| Offset   |         0 | constant nonzero mean, small sd             | none                         |

No country carries a persistent constant offset.
The deflation reproduces official growth for 56 of the 75 countries.

** Spike: one-time discontinuity at an exchange-rate regime change

The GDP level jumps once, by a factor close to the exchange-rate change.
Myanmar rises from 20,387 in 2011 to 2,485,941 in 2012, a factor near 122,
against $\Delta\ln e = -4.77$; the resulting $g^{IO} = 4.80 \approx -\Delta\ln e$.
Nigeria rises from 881,635 in 1998 to 3,345,492 in 1999, a factor near 3.8,
against $\Delta\ln e = -1.44$.
The raw national tables re-express values at the new official rate in the break
year, and the level discontinuity enters the growth rate once.
Domestic inflation does not account for the jump (Myanmar 2012 $\pi = 0.031$).

** Volatile: mean-zero annual dispersion

The gap fluctuates year to year with a mean near zero and a large standard
deviation, so the multi-year average growth is right while single years err.
New Zealand has a mean gap of 0.002 with a standard deviation of 0.038, swinging
between $+0.058$ and $-0.068$ across adjacent years.
The regime concentrates in transition and emerging economies (Viet Nam, Ukraine,
Bangladesh, Kazakhstan, Bulgaria, Romania), which points to year-to-year noise in
the component deflators or import shares of those IO tables, not a fixed bias.

* Decision

The diagnosis has answered its causal question, and the productive work shifts.
The remaining spec tasks built on the exchange-rate-versus-inflation framing
(counterfactual, residual attribution across H2 to H4, confidence statement) are
no longer the object, because that framing is resolved.
The next work is a per-country and per-year gap audit whose target is to drive
the gap toward zero across the base, treating each gap above a threshold as a
defect and separating the regimes above.
The 56 clean countries need no further work.
The 7 spike countries require detecting and splicing the exchange-rate-regime
level breaks in the raw tables.
The 12 volatile countries require tracing the year-to-year dispersion into the
component deflators and import shares of their IO tables.
