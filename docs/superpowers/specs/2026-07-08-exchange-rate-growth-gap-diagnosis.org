#+title: Diagnosis Plan: Exchange Rate and the GDP Growth-Rate Gap
#+date: 2026-07-08
#+filetags: :IODefl:diagnosis:
#+category: paper

* Problem statement

Real GDP growth rates computed from the deflated national input-output tables (henceforth $g^{IO}$) diverge from official real GDP growth rates (henceforth $g^{off}$).
The working conjecture is that the divergence originates in the conversion of local-currency values into United States dollars.
This document specifies a diagnosis executable with =/diagnose=.
It states one falsifiable prediction, arranges competing explanations so each admits a discriminating test, reports the current confidence, and defines the order of information that raises confidence to the 0.95 threshold.

* Scope and preconditions

The object =etc/Modelo/NIOTs_resultados.rdata= is supplied out-of-band by the maintainer.
The plan therefore begins at reproduction from the supplied object, not at its reconstruction.
The benchmark decision (World Bank constant United States dollars versus United Nations value added at constant prices) is left open and resolved empirically in Hypothesis H4.
The primary benchmark for the central prediction is World Bank series =NY.GDP.MKTP.KD= (=inputs/world_bank/GDP_constant=), which converts at a fixed base-year exchange rate.

* Mechanism traced in code

GDP is constructed on the expenditure side in =prepare_data()= at =code/support_functions.R:155=:

\[
\text{GDP}_t = \sum_{k \in \{C, I, E, G, X\}} (1 - m_{k,t})\, F_{k,t}
\]

where $F_{k,t}$ are final-demand components and $m_{k,t}$ import shares.
The IO growth rate is $g^{IO}_t = \Delta \text{GDP}_t / \text{GDP}_{t-1}$ at =code/support_functions.R:202=.

Each component $F_{k,t}$ is converted by =deflator_geral=, constructed in =code/tmp.R:236= as $e_t / P_t$ (year-varying nominal rate $e_t$, deflator $P_t$).
The conversion is applied to the components in =code/deflate_tables.R:94=.
That line currently reads =Jull <- Meepo # * deflator=, so the multiplication is commented out.

Two consequences follow.
First, a year-varying rate, when applied, enters the growth rate rather than cancelling.
Second, the deflation state of the supplied =NIOTs_resultados.rdata= is unknown, because the object may have been produced by a version in which the conversion was, or was not, applied.
Whether the series is real or nominal is therefore an empirical question resolved in Rung 1 and by Hypothesis H0.

* Central prediction

Under the traced mechanism, in logarithmic approximation:

\[
g^{IO}_t \approx \underbrace{g^{realLCU}_t}_{\text{real growth in local currency}} + \underbrace{\Delta \ln(e_t)}_{\text{exchange-rate term}}
\]

The World Bank constant-dollar series converts at a fixed base-year rate $e_0$, so its growth contains no exchange-rate term:

\[
g^{off}_t \approx g^{realLCU}_t
\]

Subtracting yields the falsifiable prediction:

\[
\text{Gap}_t \equiv g^{IO}_t - g^{off}_t \approx \underbrace{\Delta \ln(e_t)}_{\text{predicted, slope } 1} + \underbrace{r_t}_{\text{deflator / coverage residual}}
\]

Because the deflation state of the series is unknown, the discriminating specification adds domestic inflation $\pi_t = \Delta\ln(P_t)$ as a second regressor:

\[
\text{Gap}_{c,t} = \alpha + \beta_e\,\Delta\ln(e_{c,t}) + \beta_\pi\,\pi_{c,t} + r_{c,t}
\]

The coefficient pattern identifies the state of the series:

| Deflation state of the series | $\beta_e$ | $\beta_\pi$ |
|-------------------------------+-----------+-------------|
| Real dollars, $e/P$ applied | 1 | 0 |
| Nominal local currency, nothing applied | 0 | 1 |
| Nominal dollars, $\times e$ only | 1 | 1 |

The exchange-rate hypothesis H1 is confirmed as dominant only in the first row, with $\beta_e \approx 1$ and $\beta_\pi \approx 0$.

Because both $g^{IO}$ and $g^{off}$ contain $g^{realLCU}_t$, that term differences out of the gap.
The correlation between currency depreciation and real contraction therefore does not confound the central regression, provided deflator and coverage coincide.
It enters only through the residual $r_t$, addressed by H2 through H4.

* Pre-check: exchange-rate unit direction

The comment at =code/price_deflator.R:78= states the rate is United States dollars per unit of local currency.
If $e_t$ is dollars per unit of local currency, depreciation lowers $e_t$, so $\Delta\ln(e_t) < 0$ and $g^{IO}$ understates growth.
This pre-check fixes the sign of the central prediction and executes before any regression.

Verification procedure:
For the United States the series should equal one or a constant across years.
For other countries the values should be small numbers below one, not large ones.
For a country with a documented depreciation episode (for example Brazil 2015, Russia 2014, Argentina) the series should fall in the episode year.
A failure of any of these three conditions inverts or invalidates the predicted sign and halts the diagnosis for a unit correction.

* Hypotheses and discriminating tests

| # | Hypothesis | Discriminating test | Prior |
|---+------------+---------------------+-------|
| H0 | The supplied series is nominal, so the gap is domestic inflation $\pi_t$, not the exchange rate | Two-regressor fit; expect $\beta_\pi \approx 1$, $\beta_e \approx 0$ | high, given the commented conversion at =deflate_tables.R:94= |
| H1 | Year-varying dollar conversion injects $\Delta\ln e_t$ into growth | Two-regressor fit; expect $\beta_e \approx 1$, $\beta_\pi \approx 0$ | primary |
| H2 | Deflator choice, OCDE versus World Bank fallback | Residual after removing $\Delta\ln e$ is larger for World-Bank-fallback countries | live |
| H3 | Chain versus fixed-base index method | Residual grows with horizon length, the signature of substitution bias | live |
| H4 | GDP definition, value added net of taxes versus GDP at market prices | Wedge is near-constant; it shrinks when the benchmark is United Nations value added rather than World Bank GDP | live |
| H5 | Sector aggregation to seven sectors | Ruled out by construction, since a country-level deflator cannot bias the GDP total; one spot check confirms | low |
| H6 | Base-year choice, 1995 versus 2010 or 2015 | Ruled out by construction, since fixed-base scaling is growth-invariant; one check confirms | low |

H5 and H6 cannot generate a growth-rate gap under the current construction.
They are stated to be excluded cheaply, which narrows the field before the costly tests.

* Current confidence and the order of information

Current confidence in H1 as the dominant cause is below the 0.95 threshold.
Nothing is measured yet, and a real confounder exists in that currency crises coincide with real contractions.
The following rungs acquire information in the order that raises confidence.

| Rung | Information acquired | Effect on confidence |
|------+----------------------+----------------------|
| 0 | Load supplied =NIOTs_resultados.rdata=; emit $g^{IO}$ for at least one country | precondition; untestable without it |
| 1 | Panel of $\text{Gap}_{c,t}$; classify the series as real or nominal by inspecting a fixed-price identity year | establishes the phenomenon and resolves H0 versus H1 at the first order |
| 2 | Two-regressor fit $\text{Gap} \sim \Delta\ln e + \pi$ with coefficients and within-$R^2$ | large increase if $\beta_e \approx 1$ and $\beta_\pi \approx 0$ |
| 3 | Counterfactual re-run with fixed base-year rate $e_0$; test whether the gap collapses | near-definitive, since it is an intervention rather than a correlation |
| 4 | Residual attribution: World-Bank-fallback subset for H2, horizon dependence for H3, United Nations value-added benchmark for H4 | rules out rivals; reaches or exceeds 0.95 |

Rung 3 is the decisive step.
Rebuilding the series with a constant $e_0$ and observing the gap vanish is the fix-and-regression-test step that a correlation alone cannot replace.
It also settles the confounder, since an intervention on the exchange-rate channel isolates its causal contribution.

* Mapping to /diagnose

Rungs 0 and 1 correspond to reproduce and minimise.
Hypotheses H1 through H6 correspond to hypothesise.
Rungs 2 through 4 correspond to instrument.
Rung 3 corresponds to fix and regression-test.

* Deliverables

- A reproducible panel =Gap_{c,t}= joining $g^{IO}$, $g^{off}$, and $\Delta\ln e_{c,t}$.
- The unit-direction pre-check result.
- The central regression estimates $\hat\beta$, $\hat\alpha$, within-$R^2$.
- The counterfactual gap under fixed $e_0$.
- The residual attribution across H2 through H4.
- A closing confidence statement referenced to the 0.95 threshold.
