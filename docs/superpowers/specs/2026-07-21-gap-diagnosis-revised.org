#+title: Spec: Gap diagnosis on the current benchmark
#+date: 2026-07-21
#+filetags: :IODefl:diagnosis:spec:

* Problem statement

Real GDP growth computed from the deflated OECD Inter-Country Input-Output
tables, $g^{IO}$, diverges from official real GDP growth, $g^{off}$.
The difference is the Gap.
The exchange-rate conjecture that motivated the first diagnosis is refuted, and
the diagnosis that replaced it was estimated against a benchmark that is eight
years out of date and four years short.
This specification restates the diagnosis on the current benchmark and defines
the order of evidence that raises each surviving hypothesis to ninety-five
percent confidence.

* Standing rules

No hypothesis is decided below ninety-five percent confidence.
Below the bar, the specification states what evidence would raise it and stops.
No diagnostic assumption is acted upon without being stated first.
No data is downloaded; every input must already be on disk.

* Preconditions and established facts

Do not re-derive the following.

The panel and the supplied file measure the same $g^{IO}$: the median absolute
difference between =results/diagnostics/gap_panel.rds= and =test/g_GDP.xlsx= is
0.013 percentage points, consistent with the two-decimal rounding of the sheet,
with no country systematically divergent.
The identity between the =dif_g_GDP= sheet and its two components holds exactly.
An earlier revision of that file was erroneous and nothing was concluded from it.

The exchange rate and inflation are refuted as causes.
The index-number formula, the base year, the within-country component
dispersion, and the import adjustment are refuted as causes; the import
adjustment reduces the gap.

The applied deflator is the OECD Economic Outlook, edition 116, measure gross
domestic product at market prices, for forty-seven countries, and the World Bank
=NY.GDP.DEFL.ZS= for the remaining twenty-nine.
Both are local-currency indices rebased to unity in 1995 and both reach 2020.
The description of the OECD series as a consumer-price measure at
=etc/Modelo/base/deflator.R:5= is incorrect.

Every drifter and every spike country is a World Bank fallback country, absent
from the OECD deflator, so deflator provenance is collinear with drift.

* The defect this specification corrects

=code/diagnose_xr_gap.R:40-41= reads
=inputs/world_bank/GDP_constant/API_NY.GDP.MKTP.KD_DS2_en_csv_v2.csv=, which
carries the release date 2018-03-01 and holds data only to 2016.
The current-dollar file used by =decompose_gap()= has the same release and the
same truncation.
The panel therefore spans 1996 to 2016, and the years 2017 to 2020 have never
been examined although the tables cover them.

The current benchmark is =test/g_GDP.xlsx=, sheet =g_GDP_WB=: the same indicator,
=NY.GDP.MKTP.KD=, downloaded in the week of 2026-07-14, covering seventy-four
countries over 1996 to 2020.
An exhaustive search of the repository established it as the only source of
official gross domestic product on disk reaching 2020.
The World Bank files and every United Nations System of National Accounts file
stop at 2016; =ICESHRE.csv= is OECD import content of exports; the World Bank
=_original= extracts hold the deflator alone; =tmp= and =outputs= do not exist
although =code/*.R= references them.

* Tasks

Each task states its verification.
A task is not complete until its verification has been run and its output read.

** Task 1: repoint the benchmark

Change =get_gOff()= to read =test/g_GDP.xlsx=, sheet =g_GDP_WB=.
The sheet is in simple growth and percentage points; the diagnosis is in log
growth, so convert as $\ln(1 + g/100)$ rather than comparing conventions
implicitly.
Retain a switch that reproduces the former benchmark, so the vintage effect
remains measurable rather than discarded.

Verify: the rebuilt panel spans 1996 to 2020; the benchmark growth reproduces
the sheet to the sheet's rounding; and the count of country-years increases by
the expected number of added years.

** Task 2: measure the vintage effect before absorbing it

With both benchmarks available, quantify per country how much of the previously
measured gap was benchmark revision.
Attend to the concentrated countries: Côte d'Ivoire, Luxembourg, Malta, Romania,
Cambodia, Saudi Arabia and Brunei.

Verify: the per-country difference between the two benchmarks is tabulated, and
its aggregate dispersion reproduces the standard deviation of 1.07 percentage
points already measured.

** Task 3: re-estimate the whole diagnosis on the full window

Re-run, on the new panel, the classification into clean, volatile and spike
regimes; the decomposition into nominal-aggregate and deflator mismatch; and the
per-country attribution by covariance with the Gap.
Treat every previously reported figure as provisional, including the split of
forty-five and fifty-five percent and the drifter shares for Ukraine, Brunei,
Kazakhstan, Viet Nam and Egypt.

Verify: the decomposition identity holds to machine precision on the new panel;
and the re-estimated taxonomy is reported beside the former one, so that changes
are attributable to the benchmark rather than to an unstated change of method.

** Task 4: examine the four unexamined years

The years 2017 to 2020 have never been analysed.
Establish whether they behave like the estimated window or introduce a regime of
their own, and treat 2020 separately, since a pandemic year strains any
fixed-weight aggregation.

Verify: the gap distribution for 2017 to 2020 is reported against the
distribution for 1996 to 2016, and 2020 is reported alone.

** Task 5: the pre-committed deflator swap

For the forty-seven countries covered by the OECD deflator, two independent
local-currency deflators exist on the same base and window, so the swap requires
no new data.
Substitute =NY.GDP.DEFL.ZS= for the Economic Outlook series and recompute the
gap.

The decision rule is fixed before the test runs and is not renegotiated
afterwards.
A change in the mean absolute gap below about twenty percent refutes the
applied-deflator channel for these countries.
A change above about fifty percent establishes it.
Between the two, no claim is made.

Two limits are stated with any result.
The test conflates provider and vintage and cannot separate them.
The substituted deflator shares a provider with the benchmark, so part of any
contraction is mechanical rather than evidence of correctness.

Verify: the pre-registered figure is reported against the threshold whatever it
is, and the two limits are restated in the finding.

** Task 6: the drifters, which the swap cannot reach

The swap cannot test the drifters, since none is covered by the OECD deflator.
A third source is required.
On disk there is =inputs/price_deflators/unsd_snaAma_GDP_ImplicitPriceDeflator_USD.txt=,
covering two hundred forty-five countries and all five drifters, which ends in
2016 and is expressed in United States dollars and therefore reintroduces the
exchange rate.

State this constraint before proposing a test.
If the United Nations series is used, either compare in dollar space on both
sides or divide out the same exchange rate the deflation applies, so the
currency term cancels rather than confounds, and restrict the window to 2016.
Any alternative source requires the maintainer's authorisation, since no data
may be downloaded.

Verify: the treatment of the currency basis is stated explicitly in the finding,
and the restricted window is reported.

* Hypotheses and the evidence that closes them

- H-nominal, the OECD final-demand aggregate departs from official nominal gross
  domestic product.
  Closed by Task 3 on the full window, if the attribution is stable across the
  two benchmarks.

- H-F, the applied deflator differs from the deflator implicit in the official
  series.
  Closed for the OECD-covered countries by Task 5 against the pre-registered
  rule; open for the drifters until Task 6 supplies a third source.

- H-G, price-concept mismatch.
  Largely refuted: the premise that the applied deflator was a consumer-price
  measure was a stale comment.
  Reopening it requires evidence from the data, not from a comment.

- H-vintage.
  The benchmark component is settled by Task 2.
  The deflator component, Economic Outlook 116 against the 2023 edition of the
  input-output tables, is untested; separating it from a genuine deflator error
  requires a second deflator vintage, which is not on disk.

- H-D, the residual is inherited from the source volumes.
  Not decidable by concentration evidence, which cannot distinguish an inherited
  defect from an applied-deflator choice.
  Closed only by Tasks 5 and 6 jointly.

* Success criteria

The panel spans 1996 to 2020 against the current benchmark.
Every previously reported figure is either reproduced on the new panel or
withdrawn.
The share of the former gap attributable to benchmark revision is quantified per
country.
Each hypothesis above carries either a verdict at ninety-five percent confidence
or a written statement of the evidence that would produce one.

* Risks

Re-estimating against a benchmark that shares a provider with the substituted
deflator invites mechanical contraction; Task 5 states this limit rather than
resolving it.
The supplied sheet is rounded to two decimals, which bounds the resolution of
any claim made from it.
Seventy-four countries appear in the sheet against seventy-five in the panel.
The single absent country is Myanmar, which is the hybrid case: a 2012
exchange-rate break over a volatile pre-2012 segment.
Repointing the benchmark therefore drops the one country whose regime is not
purely of one type, and does so silently unless the join is checked.
Report Myanmar's exclusion explicitly, and decide whether to carry it on the
former benchmark rather than lose it.
