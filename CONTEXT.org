#+title: CONTEXT: Deflating WIOD Tables / Growth Decomposition
#+filetags: :IODefl:context:

This repository decomposes real GDP growth into domestic and import-content
contributions across countries, and diagnoses why the growth computed from the
deflated input-output tables diverges from official growth.
This file is the glossary of terms specific to that domain.

* Language

** Input-output source
The national input-output tables analysed are the OECD Inter-Country
Input-Output (ICIO) tables, read from =inputs/NATIODOMIMP/= (1995 to 2020, 76
countries) and deflated by the Stage-A pipeline in =etc/Modelo/base/=.
The repository name and the upstream code refer to the World Input-Output
Database (WIOD, 2016 release); WIOD is /heritage/ only, retained for reference in
=results/= and in =code/price_deflator.R= and =code/checks.R=, and is also the
licensing-permitted source for the shippable package sample (ADR-0003).
The variable =WIOD_countries= in =global_variables.R= is misnamed: it holds the
OECD country codes.
_Avoid_: calling the analysed volumes "WIOD".

** IO growth ($g^{IO}$)
Real GDP growth computed from the deflated OECD ICIO tables, built on the
expenditure side in =prepare_data()=.
The diagnosis measures it as a log-difference $\Delta\ln \text{GDP}$, consistent
with the log-difference used for official growth.
The report pipeline (=decompose_growth()=, =support_functions.R:202=) instead
uses the simple growth $\Delta \text{GDP}/\text{GDP}_{-1}$ of the same GDP level;
the two differ at second order and diverge by several points for volatile
countries.
_Avoid_: model growth, computed growth.

** Official growth ($g^{off}$)
Benchmark real GDP growth from an external source, primarily the World Bank
constant-US-dollar series =NY.GDP.MKTP.KD=, which converts at a fixed base-year
rate.
_Avoid_: true growth, actual growth.

** Gap
The difference $g^{IO} - g^{off}$ for a country-year, the object the diagnosis
explains.

** Operational gap threshold
The absolute gap, in percentage points, below which a country-year counts as
having the gap zeroed for the purpose of the majority objective.
It is set at the knee of the coverage curve over the majority panel, the point
past which each additional 0.05 percentage points of threshold admits fewer than
three percentage points of country-years, and equals one half of a percentage
point.
At that threshold sixty-two percent of majority country-years and seventy percent
of majority countries, judged by each country's median year, clear the gap.
The deliverable is the coverage curve, not the scalar; the curve is
=figs/gap_coverage_curve.pdf= and the scalar is =GAP_THRESHOLD_PP= in
=archive/diagnose_xr_gap.R=, with =gap_coverage()= producing the sweep.
_Avoid_: tolerance, acceptable error.

** Quarantine set
The minority of country-years withheld from every pooled majority statistic and
reported separately, fixed once and cited rather than re-derived per table.
It comprises seven /spike/ countries carrying a one-time multiplicative
re-expression of every final-demand component (Myanmar, Nigeria, Malta, Cyprus,
Belarus, Cote d'Ivoire, Pakistan), five /cumulative drifter/ countries carrying
a deflator-driven drift (Ukraine, Brunei, Kazakhstan, Viet Nam, Egypt), and the
year 2020 as a distinct pandemic regime.
The seven spikes determine the pooled attribution when included, so the exclusion
is a precondition of any majority verdict, not a robustness variant.
The canonical list is =QUARANTINE_ISO= and =QUARANTINE_YEARS= in
=archive/diagnose_xr_gap.R=, applied by =majority_panel()= and =quarantine_panel()=.
_Avoid_: outliers, bad countries.

** Deflation state
Whether the supplied input-output series is real (the $e/P$ conversion applied),
nominal in local currency (nothing applied), or nominal in dollars (multiplied
by $e$ only).
It is unknown for a supplied object and resolved empirically, not assumed.

** WB-fallback country
A country whose exchange-rate or deflator series is taken from the World Bank
because it is absent from the OECD source.
The subset is the discriminating variable for Hypothesis H2.
The complement is the /OECD-covered country/, of which there are forty-seven in
the deflator source.
Every country carrying a cumulative gap drift is a WB-fallback country, so
deflator provenance is collinear with drift and the two cannot be separated
within the drifter set.
_Avoid_: missing country, imputed country.

** Reference implementation
The co-author's Stage-A project in =etc/Modelo=, which produced the supplied
=NIOTs_resultados.rdata= on the co-author's machine.
It has never been executed in this repository and is not maintained here; it is
consulted to establish what the deflation did.
Its internal inconsistencies are not defects of this project (ADR-0012).
_Avoid_: the original pipeline, the runnable version.

** Working codebase
The functional code in =code=, derived from the reference implementation.
Its analysis layer serves both the reports and the diagnosis; its Stage-A
deflation is not yet restored.
All new work belongs here.
_Avoid_: the refactor, the refactor in progress.

** Canonical data tree
=inputs=, a strict superset of the spreadsheets duplicated inside the reference
implementation.
The duplicate copies belong to the reference project and are not a second source
of truth (ADR-0012).

** Applied deflator
The price index $P$ the Stage-A deflation divides by, distinguished from the
official deflator implicit in the benchmark series by four attributes: its
provider, its price concept, its base year, and its vintage.
For OECD-covered countries the provider is the OECD Economic Outlook, edition
116, measure /gross domestic product, market prices, deflator/; for WB-fallback
countries it is the World Bank =NY.GDP.DEFL.ZS=.
Both are local-currency indices rebased to unity in 1995.
The comment at =etc/Modelo/base/deflator.R:5= describes the OECD series as a
consumer-price measure; that description is incorrect and is not the data.
_Avoid_: consumer-price deflator, the deflator.

** ICIO nominal aggregate
The nominal-dollar GDP aggregate implicit in the raw OECD ICIO tables, built on the
expenditure side as total final demand net of import content,
$\sum_j (1 - m_j) F_j = \text{Ft}_{\text{total}} - \text{M}_{\text{total}} =
\text{Fn}_{\text{total}} - \text{M\_I}_{\text{total}}$, before any deflation.
It is the one term of the identity $g^{IO} = \Delta\ln(\text{ICIO nominal, USD}) +
\Delta\ln e - \pi$ that no substitution can reach, so it is where Hypothesis H1 locates
the residual majority gap.
Its divergence from official nominal GDP is the object of map #13.
_Avoid_: nominal GDP, the aggregate.

** Identity closure
The decisive test of the pipeline-error audit (map #8, ticket #11): reconstructing the
ICIO nominal aggregate independently from the raw tables for every country-year and
checking $g^{IO}(\text{deflated}) = \Delta\ln(\text{nominal}_{\text{raw}}) + \Delta\ln e
- \pi$ cell for cell.
It closed to a residual of order $10^{-15}$ log points, so $g^{IO}$ is a faithful
transform of the raw aggregate and no Stage-A or aggregation error masquerades as H1.
On that basis H1 is confirmed at ninety-five percent on a /verified-pipeline basis/
rather than by elimination alone.
_Avoid_: the reconciliation, the sanity check.

** Inherited defect
A gap component originating in the supplied OECD volumes themselves, which no
change to this repository can remove.
It is distinguished from an /applied-deflator choice/, a gap component
originating in which price index the pipeline elects to divide by, which is
removable here.
The two produce the same statistical signature under concentration-by-country
tests and are separable only by re-deflating with an alternative index.
_Avoid_: source data quality, data problem.

** Exchange-rate unit convention
The rate $e$ is expressed as US dollars per unit of local currency, so a
depreciation lowers $e$.
The OECD input file stores the inverse (local-currency units per US dollar, US
value one) and is inverted on read.
_Avoid_: FX, parity.
